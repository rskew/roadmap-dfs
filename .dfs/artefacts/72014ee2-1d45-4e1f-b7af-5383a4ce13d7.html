A veil of 90% keeps text readable on any picture

The page draws the picture under a layer of the page's own paper colour. The node assumes a veil of 90% (the picture shows at 10%) keeps the text readable whatever the picture is. Move the strength and see the worst case: a picture pixel of pure black or pure white behind the text.

Which mode to look at

Rule on this node: what the work takes to be true is settled by the choice above.

This page is also the answer to a raise. Choose above, then send it.

The input it depends on

Left: over a black pixel. Right: over a white one. Text is the page's body text and its muted text.

What would show it wrong

Wrong if body text falls under 7:1 or muted text under 4:1 over a pure black or pure white pixel at a 90% veil, in either mode, or if at 90% the picture is too faint to see.

The evidence so far

For: computed from the page's own palettes (light paper #f5f4ef, ink #1a1d21, muted #676869; dark paper #171a1d, ink #e9e7e1, muted #a09f9c). At 90%, body text is 12.3:1 over black and 15.5:1 over white in light mode, 14.5:1 and 10.6:1 in dark mode.

Against: muted text, which sits exactly at 4.5:1 on plain paper, drops to 4.1:1 in light mode over a pure black pixel at 90%; it reaches 4.5:1 only at about 94%. Real pictures are rarely pure black behind small text.
