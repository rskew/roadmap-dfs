Counted by docs/dfs-round-bound-by-size.py over the transcripts on this box (292 reviewed tasks; dollars are estimates where the runner recorded none). The four replayed tasks (W22, W11, W5, W36) each had exactly one issues review in their original run. A task with several issues rounds costs more, so a cap on cost keeps the one-round tasks and drops the rest: the 1 in 16 is a property of the cut, not of the loop.

What would show it wrong

The assumption is wrong if tasks of the replays' size have as many second rounds as the loop at large. They do not: 3 of 181 cheap tasks against 58 of 111 dear ones. The cheap tasks hold 0.1% of the spend after a second issues review; the dear ones hold 99% of it.
