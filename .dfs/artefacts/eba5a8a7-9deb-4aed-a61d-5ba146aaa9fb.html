If the judge truly flagged 75% of backtracks and 25% of controls, 12 against 12 finds the gap 77% of the time. At 60% against 30% it finds it 36% of the time. So 12 against 12 can show a large gap and not a small one.

Reading the critics' reasons afterwards: 7 of the 12 rested on running something (a flow at the earlier commit, a script, a real kiro or claude binary) or on state outside the commit. 5 could be seen by reading, and the judge flagged 2 of those.

A judge with a shell could try the 7, and costs more per node, and can change the copy it runs in. The gate E2 asks of a worker is words, which a reader can check against files; running the worker's checks again is a different gate.
