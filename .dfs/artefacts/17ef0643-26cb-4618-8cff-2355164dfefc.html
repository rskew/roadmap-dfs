Four ways to explore a code explanation

All four show the same real facts about scripts/inbox.py (the hook that hands the author's messages to a running agent), drawn from its call tree and notes. Pick a way and use it, then choose at the foot. Today: one long page, the whole call tree and then six lens sections one after another.

    A · Lens lights: the tree stays, you pick the angle

    Tap a lens. Functions with nothing to say in that lens fade, the rest light up, and only that lens's sentences are listed below, in call order. Nothing new for the worker to write.

    B · Function card: tap a function, see all six lenses on it

    The opposite cut: one function at a time with everything known about it, and who calls it. Nothing new for the worker to write.

    C · Scenario steps: walk one case through the code

    Case: two hooks fire at once and one message, 001.txt, is waiting. Step through it and watch the queue, the stored state and the open resources change. The worker writes the steps for each case by hand.

    The same grammar as today's page: one colour and letter per lens (D data, S state, R resources, F failure, Q queue, E errors handled), and every sentence comes from a hand-written note the generator checks against the code.
