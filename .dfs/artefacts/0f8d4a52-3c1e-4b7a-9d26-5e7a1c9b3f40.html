Decisions in force: by hand or derived? (second look)

Both ways give a task one cited line per standing fact. They differ in where the line lives and who keeps it true. The first look compared hand-kept lines with change-style derived lines. This time the derived lines are written as standing facts, on the same W75 (the longest task: 69 nodes, 68 confirmed). Switch between the ways.

The session that confirms a node edits the line in the status file's Decisions in force. A line may restate a fact and cite the chain of nodes that shaped it.
↓ checked by three rules in check.py
Cited node exists and is confirmed; at least one cited node is not superseded; a commit that makes a cited node superseded must rewrite the line.

Works for old tasks: the 15 lines of W75 were written once at the move, from the history. No node had to be edited.

Stale case, run on a copy of W75: a new node W75.70 with Supersedes: W75.42 leaves the accept line citing W75.42. With the rules on, check.py refuses the commit. With them off, it says clean and the line stays.

A node that settles a standing fact carries a Decision: line; a refining node says Supersedes:.
↓ generated, with no rule
The status file lists the Decision: lines of confirmed nodes that nothing supersedes. A superseded node's line drops out by itself.

Stale case: the same W75.70 retires the W75.42 line with no rule. It adds the new fact only if W75.70 wrote its own Decision: line.

Needs marks nobody wrote: the 203 tasks in the repo hold no Supersedes: except the one added to W75.37 when W75 was moved. Without marks the retiring does not happen, and nodes already confirmed cannot be edited to add them.
