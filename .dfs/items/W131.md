# W131 · Optimise the UX

## Summary

The work measured where the author's time goes from the logs as they stood on 2026-11-05: 50 raises, 21 written by the runner (since stopped) and 29 by sessions. Session raises are long (median 326 words) yet usually answered in a few words. The 6 that name an answer page were answered a little faster (median 2.2 hours against 4.9 for 18 others; a shuffle gives a gap that large 1 time in 10, so it is a lean and not a finding), and the raise template now asks for a page on a raise that offers options. Of 165 artefact pages, 127 can post a ruling and none is empty, but 38 of the 73 Hypothesis pages written since W100 never say what would show their assumption wrong. Without changing what any rule refuses, it built: a "What I need from you" opening line in the raise template (the logs cannot show it helps); a `check.py` note when a new Hypothesis page never says what would show it wrong; in the terminal screen and web page, a tree-pane rule that keeps the task, status and "12 sessions since you spoke" on screen when an opened node scrolls the title away; a closed node's "wrong if" that wraps whole and ends at the test, also under the closed web row; a `Code files` line on an opened node (cached by commit, saying how many commits it did not read); and the opened node's Approach drawn as labels and bullets instead of one run-on paragraph. The web page was then driven in headless chromium at 390 and 1280 px for the first time: no sideways scroll, unlabelled control, low-contrast text or console error, and four smaller faults were fixed: a `--no-walk` server's Chats route now says chat is off, the phone adds a task from a tab instead of a button that covered the rows' badges (31 of 220 scroll positions), the fold button is 44 px, and the Accept dialog's note box shows a shadow when it scrolls. A one-page decision view and a link to the code explainer were left unbuilt: explainer notes exist for 3 files (8% of node commits), and on W75, W130 and W97 the task page shows every "Wrong if" but a node's evidence is one tap per assumption and the Accept dialog shows none, so putting the evidence in the dialog is the open next step and not built. The artefact pages were then drawn for the first time (the earlier "blank" reading was missing fonts): none is blank or has a console error, 46 keep a chosen option over a reload, and three faults the text dump could not show were fixed in the pages: 7 had no viewport meta and drew at 980 px, 4 W75 pages drew wider than a phone because one test name would not wrap, and one chat page drew both outcomes so its buttons did nothing. The web page was also driven through answering a raise, the add-node form, reorder and the keyboard, at 320, 390 and 1280 px. The remaining failures in `test_web`, `test_chat` and two in `test_tree` fail the same without the changes; the terminal's colour, real footer and side panel stayed unseen, the walk controls and run log need a running chain, the Confirm and Refute buttons on the artefact pages were not exercised, two W1 mock-ups stay 880 px wide, and the artefact templates still have no guard against a long unbreakable word.

## Goal

Following from our optimisation of the dfs flow, do some broad research and experimentation into how we can improve the UX around task management. The goals are to enhance user understanding of assumptions and raises, and reducing the time required of the user. In other words, let the user spend less time guiding the process, but enable them to get a deeper understanding of the system in that shorter time.
In focus are things like integration and quality of artefacts, presentation of tasks and nodes, appropriateness and informativeness of raises, and possibly integration if interactive system exploration and design tools, maybe others too (be creative)
