# W131 · Optimise the UX

## Goal

Following from our optimisation of the dfs flow, do some broad research and experimentation into how we can improve the UX around task management. The goals are to enhance user understanding of assumptions and raises, and reducing the time required of the user. In other words, let the user spend less time guiding the process, but enable them to get a deeper understanding of the system in that shorter time.
In focus are things like integration and quality of artefacts, presentation of tasks and nodes, appropriateness and informativeness of raises, and possibly integration if interactive system exploration and design tools, maybe others too (be creative)

## Tree

### W131.1 · Count what the author is asked and how they answer
Status: confirmed
Approach: **The question**
- Where does the author's time go at a raise: how many are there, who writes them, how long are they, how long do answers take and how long are they?

**What this node does**
- `docs/ux-raises.py` reads every `raise` and `answer` entry in `.dfs/items` and `.dfs/archive/items` and prints the counts. Output is `docs/ux-raises.txt`.

**How we will know it worked**
- The script runs on the committed logs and its totals match a hand count of `grep -hE '^- [0-9T:Z-]+ · raise'` over both directories (49).
Determination: Of 49 raises, 21 are written by the runner (no node; median answer 2 words, "Continue" or "Commit them") and 28 by sessions (median 69 words, answered in a median 3.1 hours, only 3 with an answer page), so the biggest cheap saving is the runner's own raises, and wait time is measured, not time spent reading.

### W131.2 · Make the runner's own raises need no decision
Status: refuted
Approach: **The question**
- The runner raises for two causes: "N reviews found issues, review the tree" and "git would not merge because of your uncommitted edits". Both are answered "Continue" or "Commit them". Can the runner act on its own, or offer a one-tap answer, and leave the author only the choice that is theirs?

**Why it matters**

**What this node does**
- Read all 21 runner raises and their answers, group them by cause (11 review or work-session count, 10 blocked merge), and check the dates against the code that raises them. No code change if they have already stopped.

**How we will know it worked**
- `docs/ux-raises.txt` prints the date range of each writer's raises; `grep` of `scripts/` (not tests) for the count text finds none.
Determination: The runner's raises already stopped (all 21 are dated 2026-10-08 to 2026-10-25, the count stops are gone from `scripts/` and the blocked merge now sets the author's edits aside), so nothing is left to change here and W131.1's call that they are the biggest saving no longer holds.

### W131.8 · Let a tooling-issue raise carry on with a workaround
Status: confirmed
Approach: **The question**
- Of 28 session-written raises, 15 report a roadmap-tooling fault, and five of the author's answers say to work around it, check a task exists and carry on. Should such a raise record the issue and let the task go on when the session has a workaround, instead of stopping the task until the author answers?

**Why it matters**
- Each tooling raise blocks its task for a median of about three hours of waiting (all 49 raises: 3.2), and the same fault was raised again on other nodes.

**What this node does**
- State the change as a Hypothesis with an answer page, because it changes what §0 says a raise does and so is the author's call. Cover two parts: a non-blocking "tooling note" when a workaround exists, and a check that a raise for a known fault points at the open task for it. Do not edit `.dfs/ROADMAP.md`. Raise it for the author's ruling.

**How we will know it worked**
- The page lets the author choose between the options, and the raise carries the options in plain text.
Hypothesis: The author's five answers to tooling raises ("work around it, check a task exists, carry on") are a standing preference, so a session with a workaround may record a note and go on. Wrong if the author wants to decide each tooling fault, or a workaround would change the code the fault is about. .dfs/artefacts/277cff02-d6cc-4d1c-a298-d55cf5dca189.html
Evidence:
- for: `docs/ux-raises-classes.tsv` and the logs: W60.4, W111.13, W116.33, W123.20 and W75.60 were each answered with a work-around or check-and-carry-on instruction.
- against: the other tooling answers were a letter or digit (A, B, 2, 3), so reading those as the same preference is mine, not stated.
Determination: Options and a drawing are written and raised, and the law is left unchanged until the author rules.
