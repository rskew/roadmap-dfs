# W131 · Optimise the UX

## Summary

The work measured where the author's time goes: 49 raises, 21 written by the runner (since stopped) and 28 by sessions, with answers usually a few words. It checked that every assumption states how it could be wrong, that the old terminal-screen findings are answered in the code, and that most artefact pages carry an answer form. Nothing in the raise or assumption format needed changing. The author ruled that tooling faults keep being raised and stop their task, so the rules are unchanged. A one-page decision view per task and a link to the code explainer were left unbuilt because the existing views carried the same content and the benefit was unmeasured. The screens were checked by reading code, not seen rendered, because this sandbox draws no text.

## Goal

Following from our optimisation of the dfs flow, do some broad research and experimentation into how we can improve the UX around task management. The goals are to enhance user understanding of assumptions and raises, and reducing the time required of the user. In other words, let the user spend less time guiding the process, but enable them to get a deeper understanding of the system in that shorter time.
In focus are things like integration and quality of artefacts, presentation of tasks and nodes, appropriateness and informativeness of raises, and possibly integration if interactive system exploration and design tools, maybe others too (be creative)

**What this node does**
