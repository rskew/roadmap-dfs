# W131 · Optimise the UX

## Summary

The work measured where the author's time goes from the logs as they stood on 2026-11-05: 50 raises, 21 written by the runner (since stopped) and 29 by sessions. Session raises are long (median 326 words) yet usually answered in a few words. The 6 that name an answer page were answered a little faster (median 2.2 hours against 4.9 for 18 others; a shuffle gives a gap that large 1 time in 10, so it is a lean and not a finding), and the raise template now asks for a page on a raise that offers options. Of 165 artefact pages, 127 can post a ruling and none is empty, but 38 of the 73 Hypothesis pages written since W100 never say what would show their assumption wrong. Without changing what any rule refuses, it built: a "What I need from you" opening line in the raise template (the logs cannot show it helps); a `check.py` note when a new Hypothesis page never says what would show it wrong; in the terminal screen and web page, a tree-pane rule that keeps the task, status and "12 sessions since you spoke" on screen when an opened node scrolls the title away; a closed node's "wrong if" that wraps whole and ends at the test, also under the closed web row; a `Code files` line on an opened node (cached by commit, saying how many commits it did not read); and the opened node's Approach drawn as labels and bullets instead of one run-on paragraph. The web page was then driven in headless chromium at 390 and 1280 px for the first time: no sideways scroll, unlabelled control, low-contrast text or console error, and four smaller faults were fixed: a `--no-walk` server's Chats route now says chat is off, the phone adds a task from a tab instead of a button that covered the rows' badges (31 of 220 scroll positions), the fold button is 44 px, and the Accept dialog's note box shows a shadow when it scrolls. The Accept dialog now shows, under each assumption's "Wrong if", its first four evidence lines (a node's evidence was one tap per assumption on W75, W130 and W97; 555, 960 and 516 words against 1,759, 3,032 and 2,368 on the sheets), in a list that scrolls so Cancel and Accept stay in view; evidence moved to the archive was hidden on the node sheet too, which made old W75 nodes look evidence-free, and is now read back. A one-page decision view stays unbuilt because the dialog carries the same words, and a link to the code explainer stays unbuilt because explainer notes exist for 3 files (8% of node commits); the terminal accept prompt still shows no evidence. The artefact pages were then drawn for the first time (the earlier "blank" reading was missing fonts): none is blank or has a console error, 46 keep a chosen option over a reload, and three faults the text dump could not show were fixed in the pages: 7 had no viewport meta and drew at 980 px, 4 W75 pages drew wider than a phone because one test name would not wrap, and one chat page drew both outcomes so its buttons did nothing. The web page was also driven through answering a raise, the add-node form, reorder and the keyboard, at 320, 390 and 1280 px. The remaining failures in `test_web`, `test_chat` and two in `test_tree` fail the same without the changes; the terminal's colour, real footer and side panel stayed unseen, the walk controls, the run log and the Break and Stop buttons on Reorder rows were never measured (they need a running chain), the Confirm and Refute buttons on the artefact pages were not exercised, two W1 mock-ups stay 880 px wide, and the artefact templates now wrap a long unbreakable word (checked in the template source, not drawn). Every dialog (Answer with and without a page, Add a node, Refute/confirm, Accept) was drawn at 320, 390 and 1280 px and at 1280 x 400: before, 5 of 20 opened scrolled with the heading out of sight, because chromium scrolls the focused box into view after the dialog opens even with `preventScroll`; now the dialog is put back to its heading until the reader touches it, and all 20 open at the heading and still scroll by wheel.

## Goal

Following from our optimisation of the dfs flow, do some broad research and experimentation into how we can improve the UX around task management. The goals are to enhance user understanding of assumptions and raises, and reducing the time required of the user. In other words, let the user spend less time guiding the process, but enable them to get a deeper understanding of the system in that shorter time.
In focus are things like integration and quality of artefacts, presentation of tasks and nodes, appropriateness and informativeness of raises, and possibly integration if interactive system exploration and design tools, maybe others too (be creative)

### W131.43 · Fix: the "dialogs open at their heading" change (`preventScroll` in `form()`) probably does nothing, and nothing shows it works
Parent: W131.42
Status: confirmed
Approach: **What is wrong**
- W131.42 changed `form()` in `scripts/web/index.html` to `first.focus({ preventScroll: true })` so a dialog opens at its heading, and the Summary says the Answer dialog "no longer scrolls to its first box on opening". But `d.showModal()` runs just before it, and showModal itself focuses the dialog's first focusable element, scrolling it into view. I checked with headless chromium on a plain page (a `<dialog>` with `max-height:300px; overflow:auto` holding a heading, 600px of filler and a textarea): `dialog.scrollTop` was already 400 straight after `showModal()`, before any `focus({preventScroll:true})` call, and stayed 400 after it. In the Answer dialog without a page (`answerForm`) and in the Add-node dialog the textarea is the first focusable element, so the same happens there; the change only helps where something earlier (the Accept dialog's `ul.assume[tabindex=0]`, the answer-page iframe) takes showModal's focus first. The test `test_a_dialog_opens_at_its_heading_not_scrolled_to_its_first_box` only greps the source for the string, so it passes whether or not the dialog scrolls.

**What to do**
- Draw it: with the fixture site of `docs/ux-web-flows.py` (playwright, chromium, `FONTCONFIG_FILE` naming a font dir as in `docs/ux-web-playwright.py`) open the Answer dialog (with and without a page), the Add-node dialog, the Accept dialog and Refute/confirm at 320, 390 and 1280 px, and print each `#form`'s `scrollTop` right after it opens and whether its `<h3>` is in view.
- If any opens scrolled, fix it so the heading is in view, for example by setting `d.scrollTop = 0` after `showModal()` and the focus call, or giving the dialog's heading `autofocus`; keep `preventScroll` only if it is still needed.
- Replace the source-grep test with a check that can fail: a rendered case in a script under `docs/`, or at least a `test_web` assertion on the code that resets the scroll. Correct the Summary's sentence about the Answer dialog to what the drawing showed.
Determination: Confirmed and fixed: `preventScroll` did not hold (chromium scrolls the focused box into view 100 to 200 ms after `showModal`, and 5 of 20 dialog views opened with the heading out of sight, e.g. 192 px at 320 px wide), so `form()` now puts the scroll back until the reader touches the dialog, and all 20 open at the heading and still scroll; drawn by the new `docs/ux-web-dialogs.py` (![the Answer dialog before: heading scrolled out](.dfs/artefacts/09df0d46-94da-4f4a-bca3-e00ccff5c64e.png) ![after: opens at the heading](.dfs/artefacts/2015635c-f287-4950-b6bd-9f95ffef9319.png)), and the `test_web` case now pins the reset (still a source check), with the same four unrelated `test_web` failures as before.
