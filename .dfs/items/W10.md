# W10 · Allow customizing the theme so that different projects don't get mixed up

## Summary

Each project now has a colour of its own, kept in `.dfs/theme` as `#rrggbb` or, when none is chosen, made from the project's name so two projects differ without any setup. It is a stripe along the top of the web page's bar, the browser's and installed app's theme colour, and the project's name in the terminal header (which used to say "roadmap" in every project). It is chosen in the page's rename dialog, and clearing it returns to the colour made from the name. "Theme" was read as this identity colour and not the light/dark switch, which was already separate per project; the browser flow and the dialog's script could not be run here (no node, playwright or working chromium), so the dialog is untested in a browser.

## Goal

Allow customizing the theme so that different projects don't get mixed up

### W10.6 · Fix: the terminal's project colour collapses to six muted shades
Parent: W10.3
Status: confirmed
Approach: `scripts/tui.py` `project_attr`/`xterm256` snap the colour to the 6x6x6 cube with `round(c/255*5)`, and on a dark terminal first mix it 55% toward white. Checked: over all 360 hues of `colour_from_name` (lightness .28, saturation .55) this gives only 6 distinct xterm colours on a light terminal (60, 65, 66, 95, 96, 101: dark, dull greys-greens-browns) and 6 on a dark one (146, 151, 152, 181, 182, 187: near-white pastels), so any two auto-coloured projects share a terminal colour about 1 time in 6 and the colours are hard to tell apart even when they differ; a chosen colour like `#ff0000` becomes pale pink on a dark terminal. W10.5 claimed projects "differ in the terminal"; the name leads the header, but the colour (the Goal's point) does not reliably. Fix so the terminal colour discriminates: e.g. use true colour (`COLORTERM=truecolor|24bit`, `curses.can_change_color()` with `init_color`) when available, and for the 256 fallback pick the nearest cube/grey entry by real xterm levels (0,95,135,175,215,255) from a more saturated, less washed colour (do not lighten by a fixed 55% blend; derive a terminal colour of fixed lightness from the hue instead). Add a `test_screen.py` test that the 360 auto hues give at least, say, 12 distinct terminal colours on both light and dark terminals, which fails on today's code.
Determination: `project_attr` now takes the colour's hue at a fixed lightness (.3 on a light terminal, .65 on a dark one, full saturation) and snaps it by the cube's real levels (0,95,135,175,215,255), giving 12 distinct terminal colours on a light terminal and 24 on a dark one over the auto hues (6 and 6 before), with white-on-paper contrast of at least 3.8:1 and a chosen `#ff0000` staying red on both; the new `ProjectColour` tests in `test_screen.py` pass, a real `tui.py` in a 256-colour pty drew `roadmap-dfs` as `38;5;227` (was 187), and `check.py` is clean; true colour was left out as a 256-colour fix already separates the hues, and the 10 other `test_screen.py` errors (`curses.color_pair` before `initscr`) occur identically at the previous commit.
