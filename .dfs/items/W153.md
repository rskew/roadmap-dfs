# W153 · Next round of dfs optimisation: analyse and replay seven changes to the hypothesis workflow, weighing the author's time

## Goal

Run the next optimisation round of the dfs system (the loop of W130, `docs/dfs-optimization.md`) on the seven changes below. A change is judged on three things in this order: the quality of the delivered tree, then the author's time, then credits. The author's time was added to W130's two-tier measure on 2026-11-05 because the author's attention is the scarcest input. It is counted from the task file and log: raises (loop checkpoints and tooling faults counted apart), hypothesis pages the author would open, and the words the author reads (Summary, Determinations, Hypotheses). Say plainly if a tier ordering or a margin is a choice of yours, as a Hypothesis.

The changes, each one variable in its own arm (a bundled arm cannot say which line moved the score, W130.49). The numbering keeps the chat's; there is no E3.

1. E1 · A Hypothesis is a plain assumption line (assumption, then `Wrong if`) with no answer page. A page is made only when the worker would otherwise raise and wants the author to rule. Today `check.py` refuses a Hypothesis without a page. Also analyse, without an arm, what an `assumed` status would have done: re-status the roadmap-dfs, shop-app and farm-app hypotheses whose `Wrong if` only the author can answer, and count what the author would have read.
2. E2 · A gate at determination: before a node is confirmed, its Determination names what was observed of the thing the claim is about, and whether that observation would have come out differently had the claim been false.
3. E4 · The review loop's checkpoint stays, and becomes a decision the author can make in one line: it shows the churn evidence (findings caused or left by the previous fix, and whether findings fell between rounds) and a recommended default (continue or stop). It is not suppressed: after 15 of the 35 loop raises answered "continue", the task went on to four or more issues reviews.
4. E5 · A tooling fault found in a task is filed as a draft task and the task goes on, instead of a raise that blocks it. A raise stays for a fault that blocks the task's Goal.
5. E6 · A Determination has a word budget that `check.py` enforces, in place of "one sentence".
6. E7 · A review's minor findings go in its `ok` for the author to read, not in a "Minor findings" node of their own.
7. E8 · The reviewer is given the Goal, the rubric and the tree, and not the worker's Summary or Determinations.

Parked unless budget is left: spike-first ordering of siblings (refuted nodes mostly have no work under them), and locking a Hypothesis once its node has evidence (no edit after evidence was found in roadmap-dfs).

Method. Replays alone cannot decide most of these (see Background), so use three kinds of evidence and say which each change rests on.
- Accounting from the history, for effects that are mechanical and need no replay: E1 (pages and the tokens they cost), E5 (classify the 23 tooling raises and their answers), E6 (words per Determination), E7 (the 169 minor-findings nodes, and how many of the minor lines reappeared later as defects), E4 (the 36 loop raises: what each showed, and what followed "continue").
- Judging finished trees, with no new replay: the 20 replay trees in `.dfs/runs/replays` and the 4 in `optimisation-data/`. E2: a blind judge asks of each confirmed Determination whether its evidence observed the claim. Validate the judge first on the 25 critic backtracks and the author's 15 rulings on hypotheses as labelled cases, and report its misses and false alarms. E8: a review-only run of a blind reviewer and an informed one on the same tree, with the rubric's unmet clauses as the truth.
- Replays, as a check for harm from E1, E2, E6 and E7 (arms pooled), and for E4 and E5 where the replayed task reaches a loop checkpoint or a tooling fault. A replay stops on a raise (the chain scores BLOCKED). The running context, the session driving the replay, answers it: it logs the answer in the replay's worktree with `tree.py log <task> answer <raise ts>` and runs `replay.py run` again to resume (a resumed run works since W130.17). No change to `replay.py` is needed; the session cap counts across the resumes, so a replay keeps the cap of twice the original's sessions. Keep the rubric written first, a blind judge, and ties inside the judge's own spread (1.5 clauses). Take hypothesis-rich or dear tasks (W97, W17, W7, W21, and a shop-app or farm-app task where the box runs); a replay of cheap tasks hides the later rounds (W130.44) and averages 0.8 hypotheses a tree. E4's checkpoint only fires after several issues reviews, so it needs a dear task (W68, W74, W89, W91, W87, W116, W97 or W108); take at most two.

Answering raises in a replay. The running context knows how the task really went, and may use that to answer; a replay is comparable across arms only if the answers do not depend on the arm, and do not hand the arm the fix. So:
1. Before any arm runs, write the task's answer table from the original's log: for each raise the original author answered, its question (kind and subject) and the answer verbatim; for a raise the author never saw, the option the historical outcome took. Use only what the author knew at that raise's time and a choice among the options the raise itself offers: never the later fix, the final code or the rubric.
2. Answer a raise that matches a table entry with that entry, the same in every arm. A raise the table does not cover (a new question, or E4's new checkpoint) gets the option the original task took, or "proceed on your recommendation" when the outcome does not settle it, and is logged as flagged. Report results on unflagged runs first and flagged runs beside them.
3. Log each answer as the driver's, with its words, and count it in the author-time tier as an answer the author would have had to give. A replay has no wait between raise and answer, so that wall time is not measured. The original author's corrections at nodes cannot be mapped onto a replay's nodes and are not replayed.
4. State as a Hypothesis that a driver-answered raise stands for the author's, wrong if the same task replayed with its table answers and with a blank "proceed" answer differs on the rubric by more than the judge's spread, which would show the answers carry the fix. Run that one pair before relying on any answered replay.

Size each measure from the same-sitting controls before any pair is judged. Keep the mining scripts in `docs/`, with their output, so a critic can rerun every count below. File any tooling fault found along the way as a draft task (E5 applied to itself), not a fix inside this task.

Use more than the replayable tasks for the accounting and the judging. The history analyses take every task with substantive author input (an answer to a raise that is neither a loop checkpoint nor a tooling fault, a correction, or an accept): 32 of 153 in roadmap-dfs, 29 of 66 in shop-app, 3 of 31 in farm-app. Only 11 of the 42 replay candidates whose task files are here are among them; with raises answered by the driver, the ones with answers (W30, W3, W17, W97, W109, W68, W74, W89, W91, W87, W116) can be replayed too.

## Background

Counts from a mining pass on 2026-11-05 over roadmap-dfs, shop-app and farm-app (1,519 nodes, 124 raises). The scratch scripts were at `/tmp/dfsmine` (not in the repo, may be gone). The reading of why a critic backtracked a node is one reader's (25 backtracks); the other counts are mechanical, and the regexes are rough.

- Evidence that does not match its claim: of 25 critic backtracks, 10 were a check that could not fail or did not observe the claimed outcome, 5 measured a different object or definition, 5 covered only part of the claim, 4 were a false or unreproducible statement, 1 unclear. 8 of the 25 withdrew three or more nodes beneath. Evidence with a control (a check shown red without the change) appears in 12% (roadmap-dfs), 30% (shop-app) and 14% (farm-app) of hypotheses with evidence; 23% of hypotheses have only "for" lines.
- Assumptions only the author can answer: 90 of 127 roadmap-dfs hypotheses (71%) and 21 of 38 in farm-app have a `Wrong if` naming the author; session evidence confirms nearly all of them. Where the author ruled on a roadmap-dfs hypothesis, 10 of 15 were refuted (5 of 11 on other nodes); a biased sample.
- Pages: 104 of 127 roadmap-dfs hypotheses carry one, median 12 KB (about 3k output tokens, an estimate). The author acted on 18 hypotheses across the three projects.
- Order of writing (transcripts): of 106 roadmap-dfs hypotheses matched, 60 were first written in the same tool call as their determination; 32 had at least five work calls before it. Git cannot show this.
- Author interventions: raises answered, 50 / 59 / 14 (roadmap-dfs / shop-app / farm-app). Loop checkpoints are 15 / 21 / 0 of them, and 25 of those 36 were answered "continue" or the like, but a trigger of "no fall in defect count between the last two reviews" would have fired on 24 of 31, and after 15 of 35 of the answers ("continue" or not) the task ran on to four or more issues reviews. Tooling-fault raises are 15 / 8 / 3, often answered "ignore" or "work around". Substantive decisions are 20 / 30 / 13. The median time from raise to answer is 3 to 5 hours (90th percentile 17 to 55), wall time and not the author's effort. The loop raises fall in 17 tasks, the tooling raises in 16, the backtracks in 21: the sample is tasks, not events.
- Reading load: a Determination is a median 61 words (90th percentile 108) against the rule "one sentence". 169 of 1,519 nodes (11%) are "Minor findings" nodes, each a work session.
- No convergence: findings per review are flat across rounds (roadmap-dfs 2.6, 2.4, 2.7, 2.5 for rounds 1, 2, 3, 4 or more; shop-app 2.5, 2.6, 2.2, 1.9). Of 43 later-round defect lines classed, 9 came from the previous fix and 9 from an incomplete fix.
- The replay pool: the 36 roadmap-dfs, 7 farm-app and 1 shop-app candidates (original at most $20, first review said issues) average about 1 hypothesis a task, 26 of them author-falsifiable. Only 6 had a loop raise, 2 a tooling raise and 3 a backtrack. The critic never runs in the cheap pool (4 to 8 sessions); across all history only 49 of 250 tasks ever had a critic session, and 21 of those 49 had a backtrack, so the 25 backtracks understate the mismatch rate of tasks the critic never saw.
- Noise from the 20 existing replay trees: 0.8 hypotheses a tree (4 have none), at most 1 page, 0.45 minor-findings nodes (9 of 20 have any), no raises (a replay could not answer one), determination words 74 to 362 with a control SD of 30 to 60. Credits for one task's controls vary 1.9 to 2.2 times; the judge's spread is 0.71 clauses of 8 to 10. A change worth under about 15% of the clauses, or about 60 words, one hypothesis or one node a tree, is inside this noise on one pair. A dear replay costs 20 to 95 credits or more (W17 controls 20 to 24, its changed arm at least 94.7).
- Author input by task (substantive answers, corrections, accepts): 2 or more in 12 of 153 roadmap-dfs tasks, 11 of 66 shop-app, 2 of 31 farm-app (W6 and W92, field research, not replayable). The replay candidates among them: W3, W7, W17, W21, W87, W89, W91, W97, W109, W116, W4.
- The answers a driver would give: the 13 replay candidates that have a raise or correction hold 14 answered raises (6 loop checkpoints, 6 decisions, 2 tooling) and 9 corrections; W7 and W21 have corrections only, which a replay cannot take. That is a small table per task.
- Not mined or not testable from history: E8 and bundled changes. shop-app's transcripts are mostly codex, so write-order matching worked only for roadmap-dfs and farm-app.
